# Where we left the original

This remake plays Infocom's *Stationfall* (1987) through a port of its own 1987 parser and library, so typed play gives the original's answers word for word. The walkthrough gate holds it to that: every step of the winning route matches the original story file, text and status line. This page is the register of the places where the port knowingly says or does something else. Each one was approved by the sponsor, is marked in the code with a comment that begins `Deliberate deviation (sponsor, <date>, <ref>)`, and is listed here with what the original does. Proposals that were declined are listed too.

File references are to `wwwroot/` for the game and to the ZIL source for the original.

## The conveniences switch (D12, 25 September)

The engine came from the Planetfall port, which had grown a set of conveniences over its playtests. When Stationfall moved to its own 1987 parser, all of them fell away (see `porting/INHERITED-DEVIATIONS.md`). ***The sponsor chose to lean toward playability, keeping each convenience where Stationfall has the mechanic behind it***, all behind one switch, so the walkthrough can still be checked against the original with the switch off.

The switch is `config.conveniences` (`data/game.json`, `engine/core.js`). It is on for players. The walkthrough gate (`scripts/tests/walkthrough.mjs`) plays the route with it off and must match the original at every step, 336 of 336; a second run with it on must still win, 80 of 80. `?conveniences=0` on the page plays the original word for word. `scripts/tests/clicks.mjs` checks each item below both ways.

**Why the door notices were kept.** D12 kept them only if some door in Stationfall can change while the player is away. One can. When the ID reader opens the security door to the Armory, it queues I-SECURITY-DOOR, which shuts the door a turn or two later. The line "The security door glides shut." is printed only if the door is in sight (`<GLOBAL-IN? ,SECURITY-DOOR ,HERE>`, station.zil 3043-3049). A player who opens it and walks off south comes back to a shut door with nothing said. The other doors change only while the player is there: the spacetruck hatch, the iris hatch, which opens when the player feeds the validated form into its slot (globals.zil 744), and the airlock doors, which move only when the player works them (village.zil 1208, 1249). The auto-doors never hold a state at all (V-WALK, verbs.zil 2351-2365). So the notice covers doors only. Planetfall's version also covered containers; Stationfall's does not.

## The playtest UNDO (D18, 25 September): early playtesting only

The original has no UNDO. Stationfall is a version 3 game, and UNDO came with version 5; the original's only way back is RESTORE. Round one's testers asked for one (SF-034). ***The sponsor allowed a one-move UNDO for early playtesting only, to be taken away for the later blind rounds and never shipped in the published game.*** A checkpoint reload for players was declined, because it would spoil puzzles.

- **Scope.** Playtest only, and removed before later blind rounds and publication. It is marked in the code as `Deliberate deviation (sponsor, 2026-09-25, D18) -- playtest only; removed before later blind rounds and publication`.
- **The switch.** It is `config.playtestUndo`, separate from the conveniences switch, and **off by default everywhere**. The playtest harness turns it on with `--undo on`, and records that in the diary header and in the session line, so the stats can tell rounds with UNDO from blind rounds. On the page, only `?playtest-undo=1` turns it on. The published site ignores that query and forces the switch off (`scripts/lib/site-rewrite.mjs`), and `scripts/check-site.mjs` fails a release that could have it.
- **What it does.** Before each move, the whole game state is kept. UNDO puts it back, one level only and at no cost in turns, and says "[Previous turn undone.]". A saved position and the random stream are not taken back, as with RESTORE. At the death prompt it offers "type RESTART, RESTORE, UNDO, or QUIT", and UNDO takes back the fatal move. Click mode has an Undo button on the quick row and on the death screen, only while the switch is on.
- **With the switch off** the game is the original's. "undo" gets `[I don't know the word "undo."]`, and at the death prompt it gets FINISH's question again, both as s6.z3 says them. The walkthrough gate runs with both switches off, 336 of 336. `scripts/tests/undo.mjs` checks both ways.
- **Where.** `engine/core.js` (`zdispatch`, `undoMove`), `rules/library.js` (FINISH), `ui/ui.js`, `game.js`, `scripts/playtest.mjs`.

## Declined, kept as the original

These Planetfall conveniences were looked at and left as Infocom wrote them (triage C in `porting/INHERITED-DEVIATIONS.md`).

- **Walk-time notes.** No Stationfall exit costs much more than a step (V-WALK, 20 to 33 millichrons), so a note would never fire.
- **DIAGNOSE in hours.** DIAGNOSE keeps its own words ("well-rested ... well-fed"). Only the button carries the note above.
- **A worn pocket raising the carry limit.** That would change a rule, not explain one. The load limit is the original's 100.
- **"wait until morning" answered specially.** The original's `[I don't know the word "morning."]` is already clear.
- **Asking the robots about anything seen anywhere.** The original's ASK ABOUT and its FLOYD-ASK-ABOUT and PLATO-ASK-ABOUT answers stand. The click menus list those topics (below).
- **"X of Y" and proper-name folding.** The 1987 parser reads OF itself, and the robots' names already take no article.
- **SCORE costing no time; RESTART and QUIT asking first.** The original already does both.

## Interface, not deviations

The click layer (PLAN.md M4) changes nothing a typing player would read, so it is not a deviation. Every click is a typed command through the same 1987 parser: a menu offers only words the parser accepts, and a click sends exactly those words (`engine/zmenus.js`). The walkthrough is played by clicks as well as typed, and both match the original. Some of what the page shows comes from canon:

- **The compass aboard the Duffy is nautical** (fore, aft, port, starboard). The ship's rooms are described that way, and the words are in the dictionary (syntax.zil 20-23).
- **The clock shows only when the chronometer would tell the time**: worn, and before day 3 (V-TIME, verbs.zil 2029). After that, the status line has no time.
- **The package papers**: the three forms and the blueprints, as scanned (D4). READ keeps the original's "[This form can be found in your STATIONFALL package.]" (ship.zil 65), and the page opens the paper beside it. Form QX-17-T is shown validated once the game has validated it, and its course chart is a picture only; the player works out the course, as in 1987.
- **The keypads and dials** offer no numbers of their own. Each keypad's pad is named for what it drives, and each dial shows only its range (the jammer 0 to 1400, the timer to 100, the safe's 8000 settings).
- **No menu names a puzzle's answer.** Each thing's menu offers what its flags and its own routine answer (Take, Open, Read, Turn on and the like). The physical verbs any thing can be tried with (push, pull, turn, kick, break, climb, look under, look behind, touch, smell, scare, put on) sit in a "More…" submenu that is the same on every thing and scenery word, so kicking the barbershop mirror or scaring the ostrich is offered the same way as kicking the wall, not singled out. Two classes are on the first menu instead (playtest round 2). Push is there on every thing or scenery word named a button or a switch (SF-035), such as the log reader's button, the Comm Center's and the elevator's call button. Climb up and Climb down are there on a thing that is climbed both ways, which is the ladder (LADDER-F, globals.zil 557-561; SF-047).
- **What the text allows.** Hanging from the balloon's leash, the compass offers only Down, which is letting go (V-WALK, verbs.zil 2307); stunned, or sitting in a seat or a bed, it offers nothing. Things out of reach from the leash (UNTOUCHABLE?) are not offered to be taken. From a seat or a bed they are: the game's own "You can't reach the spacetruck hatch from the pilot seat." is what tells the player to get up. In a bed the quick row offers Get up and not Take all, since from the bed TAKE ALL refuses thing by thing (UNTOUCHABLE?, verbs.zil 3091-3100; SF-040); typed TAKE ALL keeps those refusals.
- **What each thing's routine answers** (playtest round 1). The verbs a thing's own action routine answers are read from the ZIL by `scripts/extract-answers.mjs` into `engine/answers.js`, rather than listed by hand, so the Comm Center screen offers Read (SCREEN-F), the Food Processing and Dispensing Unit offers Turn on (FPU-F), and the coffee offers Drink (COFFEE-F answers EAT, and DRINK is EAT, syntax.zil 208). A thing whose routine would say "You can't see any ... here." is not drawn: the village seen from the spacetruck, the log reader's button after the blast, the smashed mirror. A conditional exit whose flag is down (the Casino's north and up) is not on the compass, since it answers "You can't go that way." like no exit at all. An exit that answers in its own words is on the compass (SF-047). There are twenty of these, such as the Printing Plant's DOWN, "A few rungs down, you discover that the ladder passage to Level Eight is sealed off" (station.zil 2683-2685). Sleep is on the quick row once the game has said the player is tired, and whenever the player is in a bed (V-SLEEP answers "You're not tired." before then; SF-037). The robots' "Tell … to…" menus offer exactly the orders FLOYD-F and PLATO-F answer.
- **Saves and restarts.** The 1987 SAVE asked the interpreter for a file, so a player could keep several; the page keeps the last three, and RESTORE takes the newest. The restart link asks first, as the original's RESTART does (the manual, p. 9), and the saved positions outlive it. The compass labels a room by name once the player has stood in it, whatever the game's TOUCHBIT says.
