# The playtesters' leaderboard

Every full run of the game from the opening, ranked. A full run is one blind tester's session from round 7 on, played over several legs toward the 1,500-command cap; the segmented rounds 1 to 6, in which each session played one leg from a checkpoint, are left out, and so is any single leg. On 8 October the sponsor asked to see ***which kind of persona scored highest, filed the most kinds of friction, got the most frustrated and was blocked the most***, and ***how much of the friction may come from the persona each tester was given***. [How we tested playability](playtesting.md) explains the method, and [the playtests page](playtests.md) holds each run's diary and friction rows.

![One bar per full run: its negative friction rows per 100 commands, coloured by the host its legs ran in, with each run's persona tags and best score](wiki/metrics-playtest-testers.svg)

## Does the persona drive the friction?

**As far as 21 runs can show, the persona explains little of it, and the host explains most.** This is the reading after round 13; the generated figures under *What the numbers say* below follow later rounds.

- **Within one host the personas file at much the same rate.** Rounds 8 to 12 were all played by Claude Code testers with UNDO off, a different persona in each of their 15 runs: a retired schoolteacher who had never played a computer game, a speedrunner, a software tester, a busy parent, a librarian with graph paper. Their negative rows per 100 commands stayed between 1.2 and 2.1. Grouped by what each persona says of its experience (text-adventure veteran, newcomer, or neither) the means differ by four hundredths; by pace (careful, fast, or unstated) by about two tenths. Click runs filed a little more than typed runs (1.7 against 1.4), but there are two click runs to each typed one. Half of what spread there is lies between rounds, and each round brought a new build as well as three new personas.
- **Round 13's jump came with the host, not the personas.** Its three runs filed 7.5 to 8.7 negative rows per 100 commands, more than three times the highest earlier run (2.1). The same three testers, in the same sessions, filed 2.3 per 100 in their first legs on Copilot with Claude Opus 5.5, 11.0 in their later Copilot legs (during which Copilot switched some legs to GPT-6 Luna on its own), and 3.0 in the two final legs that headless Claude testers played. The personas stayed the same while the rate moved with the host.
- **The model mattered (round 13).** The sponsor ***reports that Copilot chose GPT-6 Luna for the testers on its own, and judges that the lower-capability model materially weakened them***. The records agree. Over their first 400 commands, all on Claude Opus 5.5, the three runs kept round 12's pace exactly: 22, 21 and 21 points, against round 12's 22, 21 and 17. From there round 12's runs gained 32 to 45 points by the cap (to 53, 53 and 67). Round 13's gained 5 to 26 (to 27, 47 and 38), over the later Copilot legs in which the switch happened. In those legs two runs went back to the opening after an unwinnable state (the typed run fell from 22 to 11, click A from 21 to 8), and negative friction rose from 2.3 to 11.0 rows per 100 commands. On headless Claude Opus 5.5, the final legs filed 3.0 per 100, and click A's scored 6 points in 125 commands. Which Copilot legs ran on Luna was not recorded, so this is the round's pattern, not a leg-by-leg proof.
- **Scores vary more than friction, and no persona kind explains them either.** In rounds 8 to 12 the best scores ran from 38 to 73. The three highest, 73, 73 and 71, were click runs by a software tester, a puzzle-hunt enthusiast and a game-design student who experiments with every object, three personas that try things; the lowest, 38, was a librarian who maps every room. That is a story three runs can tell, not a finding. Runs that scored higher filed slightly more friction (r = 0.4), because they met more of the game.

**What the data cannot separate.** Each of these changes along with the persona, so none of the differences above can be laid on the persona alone:

- **One run per persona.** No persona has played twice, so its effect cannot be told from that run's luck: one death in the wrong place, or one save kept too late.
- **The build changes every round.** Each round plays a newer engine, with the last round's fixes, so a round's friction is partly its build's.
- **The host and the model.** Rounds 7 to 12 ran in Claude Code (part of round 7 on Cursor); round 13 ran in GitHub Copilot, with Claude Opus 5.5 chosen, an unrecorded switch to GPT-6 Luna on some later legs, and headless Claude finishing both click runs after their Copilot tokens ran out. The work log records which host ran each leg, but not every model.
- **Typed against click.** The two modes meet different problems (only a typed tester can be misparsed), and each round has one typed run and two click runs.
- **UNDO and the course wall.** Round 7 had UNDO on. In round 13 the package papers were pictures that Copilot's testers could not read, which cost every run its opening.
- **Agent testers.** The testers are agents playing a person, not people; they may know the game from memory, and they never tire. The persona tags are fixed patterns read from each persona's sentence (`scripts/lib/playtest-leaderboard.mjs`), not anyone's judgement of the tester.

## How the figures are counted

Everything on this page is generated by `scripts/wiki-data.mjs` (through `scripts/lib/playtest-leaderboard.mjs`) from the harness's transcripts and friction rows, `playtests/stats.json` and the agent work log (`decisions/work.jsonl`), and nothing is kept by hand. The chart and the table are also in [metrics-playtest-testers.csv](wiki/metrics-playtest-testers.csv).

- **Best score** is the run's best score of 80 at any point, and its **furthest stage** the stage band that score reached (the full-run charts' bands). **Outcome** is how the run ended, from `scripts/lib/playtest-outcome.mjs`.
- **Deaths** are commands that left the player dead. **Restores** are RESTORE commands sent. **Back to 0** counts the times the score fell back to 0: a restart from the opening, or a save from the opening restored.
- **Friction rows** are all the rows the tester filed, praise included. **Kinds** is how many of the seven negative kinds it filed (blocked, bug, parse, not-understood, confusing, tedious, other). **Negative per 100** counts the rows that were not praise per 100 commands; **weighted per 100**, the frustration measure, weights them by severity (low 1, medium 2, high 3). **Blocked** counts the rows of the kind "blocked", and **praise** is the share of rows that were praise.
- **Host** is where each leg ran, from the work log's entry for it: Claude Code (a headless `claude -p` tester or a subagent), Cursor or Copilot, with the model where the entry names one. A leg the log does not place is counted as "host not recorded".
